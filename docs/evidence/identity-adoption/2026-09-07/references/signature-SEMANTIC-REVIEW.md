# Open signature: semantic and optical assessment

7 September 2026. The user now prefers the Open project v/e signature.
This additional meaning was recognised after the visual exploration; it was
not the original construction rationale. Canonical product decisions live in
the Wiki, Identity-and-entry.

## Assessment

The proposed values/education relationship is coherent with the established
identity. Aperture's supported, open space and the persistent learner place
within a widening organisation context both allow distinct purposes to coexist.
Tenant-led identity and a subordinate Project VE endorsement already express
shared infrastructure without requiring identical brand commitments.

The three relationships are resonances, not interchangeable definitions:
values/education concerns aspects of development; platform/organisation concerns
roles; individual development/institutional objective concerns purposes. Do not
assign one literal side of the slash to one fixed set of actors. Organisations
can have values and support broad development; education is part of the
platform's concern. The slash neither ranks those positions nor promises that
their objectives always align.

Semantic risks to manage in brand writing:

- “Values” can imply prescribed moral instruction. An organisation can use the
  platform for its own training objective without endorsing the platform's
  broader values framing. This does not imply the absence of platform standards.
- “Values and education” can sound like a formal expansion of VE and narrow the
  perceived offer to character education. Keep Project VE as the canonical name;
  the interpretation is underlying logic, not a new mandatory descriptor.
- Slash readings may include division, opposition, alternatives or a web path.
  Relation is the intended brand reading, not a universally decoded meaning.
- Treating all three pairs as a precise equation would introduce a false binary
  and obscure tensions between personal and institutional aims. The product must
  substantiate the relationship; the signature cannot guarantee it.
- Overexplaining the slash could make the signature depend on a story to work.
  It should still function for someone who never hears this rationale.

## Optical determination: no change justified

Re-inspected the existing 14/16/20/24/32px comparison and prior mobile/tenant
evidence. The native slash already has enough separation to read between v and
e. Its 500 weight is subordinate to the 600 letters without disappearing at the
proposed 16px text minimum. This is a visual assessment of the existing rendered
evidence, not a new empirical recognition result or real-device test.

Keep the Open treatment exactly as it is:

- Source Sans 3 letters at 600; slash U+002F at 500.
- Native slash angle, full opacity, no transform, recolouring or extension.
- Extra side spacing 0.035em; word gap 0.22em; tracking −0.018em.
- Existing mark size, 7px gap at product size, horizontal and compact arrangements.
- Existing canonical accessible name, Project VE.

Increasing weight makes the slash compete with the letters. Increasing spacing
separates v and e into opposing blocks. A custom angle or greater height turns
punctuation toward a separate emblem. None solves an observed optical defect.
The conceptual reading therefore supports retaining the existing balance.

Any later reproduction correction should respond to an observed small-size,
print or raster problem, not to a desire to advertise this meaning. A.2, palette,
type system, product surfaces and tenant grammar are untouched. No visual source
was changed, so existing browser checks remain applicable; no new rendering or
application tests were needed for this assessment. Final adoption is still pending.
